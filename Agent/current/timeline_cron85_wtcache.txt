WHAT GP_WTCACHE DOES (mm64_wtcache, gdn_pure_solve.cpp ~772 + solve_head ~917/949/965)
  Per-head, per-producer weight cache keyed by the weight's cv source pointer (b_cv = T_kj block).
  In the off-diag merge the inner Σ_k A_ik @ T_kj reuses the SAME T_kj weight across multiple (i,j)
  output blocks; on a cache HIT the packed (wt,bias) bytes are reused zero-copy (c->mm.wt/bias point
  at the cached buffer, the cv->kmajor 64-vgather + lo/hi + bias pack is SKIPPED). On a miss they are
  packed into a fresh slot (<=6 slots/head; the merge has at most 6 distinct T-weights/head). The diag
  (mm64/mm64_keepwt) is untouched; the T_ii@S merge weight (=S, unique) packs into the canonical buffer.
  Byte-identical to mm64 (the cache holds exactly the bytes gp_pack_wt_bias_hvx would write —
  PACKCHK/LEANCHK oracle). cron#77 keepwt (diag A²/A³ share weight=A) is orthogonal and still on.

VERDICT (structural)
  GP_WTCACHE does exactly what was predicted: it pulls 128 of the 768 WT-PACK long poles (the 4 merge
  cache-hit matmuls/head) off the producer critical path (20/head -> 16/head, per-producer 128 each).
  The 4 cache-hit mm/head run a short ACT->SPIN->OUT->ACC chain with NO WT-PACK leg. The remaining
  miss WT-PACK is still the single longest segment (~4.0K/inst) — the cache cuts the COUNT of poles,
  not the per-miss cost. Bottleneck stays FEED-BOUND; consumer (HMX) still idle ~65-70%. Bit-exact
  (oc 4.238e-3, PACKCHK=0, LEANCHK_LIVE max|d|=0). Next clean lever per cron#84 NEXT⑥ = renorm/acc
  (now the relatively larger remaining producer block: WT-PACK Σ fell, ACC Σ unchanged ~1.2M).
